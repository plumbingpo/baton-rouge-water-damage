AC Leak Water Damage Help in Baton Rouge

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Leave the system off until it is fixed.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Drywall Below the Air Handler

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Closet Walls and Platform

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.
